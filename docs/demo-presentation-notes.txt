0:00 - 1:00  OPENING: THE PROBLEM
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
DO:   Share the browser window, showing the ACHF Explorer home page.
SEE:  "Explore Australia's performing and visual arts, linked."

SAY:
- Australia's creative-sector records live in separate partner systems.
  AusStage covers live performance, DAAO covers artists and art, and the
  partner filters are already set up for future partners such as
  Creative Australia and ACMI.
- Each system is excellent on its own, but a researcher who wants the
  people, places and works across them has to search each one separately
  and join the dots by hand.
- ACHF is building one shared, standards-based layer across those
  collections. What you are about to see is that layer working end to
  end, with a search interface on top.
- In the next eight minutes I want to show you three things: that it
  works across partners, that the data is genuinely connected, and that
  it is built to be trusted. Then I will be straight about what is left
  to do.


---------------------------------------------------------------------------
1:00 - 2:00  WHAT IT IS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
DO:   Stay on the home page. (Optional: open a second tab at
      http://localhost:8000/docs and flick to it for a few seconds.)
SEE:  Header with AusStage and DAAO partner badges; the API docs page.

SAY:
- Partners publish their records as Linked Art JSON-LD. That is an open,
  international standard used by museums, not something we invented.
- When a record arrives, the repository validates it, stores it as a
  new version without overwriting the old one, indexes it for full-text
  search, and works out which partner it belongs to from its web address.
- Everything you see in this interface comes through an open REST API,
  documented at /docs. Any partner or researcher can build on the same
  API.
- The interface itself is a thin layer. It does not hold any data of its
  own; it reads the same public endpoints anyone else could use.

  can cite the record exactly as it stood on a given date.

- Built and tested today: Linked Art validation, versioned storage with

  and operations: hosting, deciding how partner credentials and access
  work, and mapping one partner's real export into Linked Art.
- Put simply: the hard, reusable part, which is validating, versioning
  and searching standard data, exists and is tested. What is left is
  the work of connecting it to a real partner, and that is best done
  with a partner.

8:00 - 9:00  SECTOR IMPACT AND THE ASK
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
SAY:
- Discovery: one place to find performances, people, places and works
  across collections that have never been searchable together.
- Research: open, versioned, standard data that researchers can cite and
  reuse, and that links back to each partner.
- Smaller organisations: a partner can publish standard data without
  building and running its own infrastructure.
- Durability: each record keeps a stable web identifier and its full
  history.
- Partners stay visible: records are attributed to them, link back to
  their sites, and can be corrected or withdrawn through the API.
- The ask: let's choose one pilot partner and a target date this
  meeting, and map a real sample of their records into the repository.
  That is the fastest way to turn this demo into a service.

FALLBACK PLAN
- Images missing: placeholders appear automatically. Keep going and say
  the images come from Wikimedia at view time.
- Map tiles not loading: skip the Map view and use the venue names and
  dates on each record instead. Mention that the coordinates are in the

LIKELY QUESTIONS AND SUGGESTED ANSWERS
Q: Is this real AusStage and DAAO data?
A: Not yet. These are hand-curated records modelled on real entities.
   The identifiers are illustrative, and the AusStage links go to the
   AusStage home page. The pipeline is the real part; a live partner
   feed is the next step.
Q: What does a partner need to do to join?
A: Send records as Linked Art JSON-LD through the API with credentials.
   The repository validates each record and gives clear errors when one
   fails.
Q: What happens when a partner corrects a record?
A: They publish it again. The old version is kept as a snapshot, and the
   new one becomes current and searchable. Shearing the Rams shows this.
Q: Can records be removed?
A: Yes. Authenticated delete endpoints remove a single snapshot or a
   whole record. Deleting the current snapshot promotes the newest
   remaining one.
Q: Who can see the data?
A: In this demo, reads are public through a switch that is off by
   default. In normal operation every API call needs credentials, and
   writes always do.
Q: Where would it be hosted?
A: Not decided. Today storage is the local filesystem with a SQLite
   search index; Swift object storage can be plugged in through the
   application factory. Hosting is part of the onboarding work.
Q: Why Linked Art rather than our own format?
A: It is an open community standard already used by museums, so the
   data is reusable by others and we avoid lock-in.
Q: How long until a pilot?
A: Don't promise a date here. Propose agreeing a pilot partner and a
   target date as the outcome of this meeting.
